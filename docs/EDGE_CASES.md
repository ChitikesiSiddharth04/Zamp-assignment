# Edge cases

Five, plus the happy path. The brief asked for 2–4 non-trivial ones; the criterion I used for
"non-trivial" was **the process has to behave differently for a reason a finance person would
recognise**, not because a field was blank.

Each is a real PDF in `data/samples/`, generated by `scripts/make_samples.py` in one of four
genuinely different vendor layouts — different words for the same field, different table
shapes, tax separated or embedded, and one flattened to a 300 dpi scan with noise and skew.

---

## 0 · Happy path — `01_apex_happy_path.pdf` → `AUTO_APPROVED`

Apex Cloud Systems, PO-10231 printed on the document, everything lines up.

Worth pausing on even though it is the easy case: **the invoice says $52,357.25 and the
process matches on $48,200.00.** The PO was raised net of tax, so the subtotal is the number
that gets compared. Getting that wrong is a class of error that produces a tolerance breach on
every single invoice from every vendor that states tax separately.

16 of 16 checks pass. Posted. No human involved. ~45 ms of processing.

## 1 · One PO, two invoices, and the second one goes over
`02_meridian_partial_1of2.pdf` → `APPROVED_PARTIAL`
`03_meridian_partial_2of2_overbill.pdf` → `ROUTE_TO_BUYER`

**Why it is not trivial:** it cannot be answered by looking at one document. Correctness
depends on what the process did on a previous run, which is why there is a ledger rather than
a stateless matcher.

Meridian Freight bill PO-10244 ($120,000) across a quarter.

* **First invoice, $72,500.** Inside the order, and the PO permits partial billing, so it is
  approved partially and **$47,500 stays open**. It is not treated as a tolerance failure just
  because it is not the full amount — that distinction is the whole point.
* **Second invoice, $51,900.** On its own it looks ordinary. Cumulatively it takes billing to
  **$124,400 against a $120,000 order — $3,650 past the allowed ceiling, 3.67% over.** Routed
  to Daniel Okonkwo, the named buyer on the PO, with a drafted email containing the full
  arithmetic: order value, already invoiced, this invoice, cumulative, overage.

The reasoning is that the overage may well be payable — demurrage on a delayed vessel usually
is — but it is a budget decision, not an AP one, and the process says so in those words.

This vendor also embeds tax in its line rates, so `TAX_BASIS_GROSS` fires: the comparison is
being made on a different basis than for Apex, and that is recorded rather than hidden.

**In the queue:** *Approve the overage* re-runs everything with the buyer's approval applied,
and it clears to `APPROVED_AFTER_REVIEW`.

## 2 · A scan with no PO on it — `04_borealis_scanned_no_po.pdf` → `HOLD_FOR_REVIEW`

**Why it is not trivial:** two independent difficulties stack, and the process has to respond
to each differently.

The PDF is an image. `pdfplumber` returns zero characters, so the process renders the page at
300 dpi and runs Tesseract, which comes back at **94.5% mean word confidence**. Every field
read from that page is scaled by it — the invoice number, date and total land at **84%, under
the 85% floor** — and the money tolerance on the matched PO is **halved**, because the digits
being compared are the least certain thing in the run.

Separately, the document never prints a PO number. It says *"supplied against our standing
blanket order for this quarter"*, which is exactly how this arrives in practice. So the
process infers it: Borealis has one open order, PO-10250, and $9,681 fits inside it. That is
almost certainly right — and it is still marked `PO_INFERRED`, critical, because **inference
is a good guess, not evidence, and a guess should not move money.**

**In the queue:** *Confirm readings & release*. The reviewer confirms three field reads and
the PO link against the document image, and the invoice runs through **every check again** —
it is not waved past them. It clears to `APPROVED_AFTER_REVIEW`, partial against the blanket
order, with the reviewer's name on the audit trail.

Run time ~1.5 s, essentially all of it OCR.

## 3 · The bank account changed — `05_apex_bank_change_fraud.pdf` → `BLOCKED_FRAUD_REVIEW`

**Why it is not trivial: this is the one where every commercial check passes.** PO-10255
exists, is open, belongs to this vendor, the amounts match to the cent, the goods are
receipted. A matcher that only compares numbers pays it.

Three things are subtly wrong, and no single one of them is proof:

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---|
| remit-to account ends **9902**; the vendor record says **4471** | +3 |
| the email domain is **apexc1oud.com** — digit one, not letter l | +2 |
| the printed name is **Apex Cloud System Inc.** — no *s* | +1 |
| vendor therefore resolves on name alone, with no corroborating domain | +1 |

**Score 7 against a threshold of 4 → payment frozen.** The escalation to financial controls
opens by saying the invoice *would have cleared on the numbers alone*, then instructs:
**verify the change by calling a number already held in the vendor master, never one printed
on the invoice** — because the invoice is the thing under suspicion. It also proposes freezing
the vendor rather than just this document, since a redirected-payment attempt is rarely a
one-off.

**This one cannot be released from the exception queue.** There is no button. Bank changes get
verified out of band or not at all.

## 4 · The same invoice again, dressed differently — `06_apex_duplicate_resend.pdf` → `REJECTED_DUPLICATE`

**Why it is not trivial:** a naive `WHERE invoice_number = ?` misses it. The resend is a
completely different layout — a plain statement rather than the branded invoice — and the
reference is punctuated differently: `INV 2041` where the original said `INV-2041`.

References are normalised to alphanumerics (`INV2041`) before comparison, scoped to the
resolved vendor. Blocked, linked back to the original run, and **no payable created**. The
note tells AP to answer from the original rather than reprocessing the copy.

A second layer sits behind it — `NEAR_DUPLICATE`, same vendor and same amount within seven
days on a *different* reference — for the case where a vendor genuinely re-numbers a resend.

Only invoices that created a liability are in the ledger, so a rejected invoice never blocks
its own corrected reissue.

---

## 5 · An invoice you cannot pay as sent — `07_lumen_incomplete.pdf` → `REJECTED_INCOMPLETE`

**Why it is not trivial:** the interesting part is not detecting the gap, it is what happens
next. Three things are wrong at once:

* **no invoice number at all** — you cannot pay, record or deduplicate without one
* **the line items sum to $14,700 against a stated total of $16,400** — $1,700 unexplained
* **no PO reference**, so the process infers PO-10260 and marks it as inferred

It is also the case that shows the vendor-name path doing real work: the letterhead just says
*lumen*, which matches nothing. The vendor is resolved from the remittance domain
`lumendesign.co` instead.

The output is **a drafted email to the vendor** naming each specific problem, including the
$1,700 discrepancy in the words a supplier can act on — *either the invoice is wrong or a
charge was not itemised* — and telling them a corrected invoice re-enters the queue
automatically. That is the difference between removing work and moving it.

## Running them

Left rail → click any of the seven. Or, from a terminal:

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/smoke_test.py
```

which runs all seven plus both human-in-the-loop paths and asserts every outcome, including
that cumulative billing on PO-10244 lands at exactly $124,400.

## Things also handled, without a dedicated sample

Closed and cancelled POs, a PO that belongs to a different vendor, currency mismatch, an
on-hold vendor (`V-1005` is in the master for this), future-dated invoices, invoices over 180
days old, invoices above the $75,000 straight-through ceiling, and — when a second extractor
is configured — the two readers disagreeing on a field.
